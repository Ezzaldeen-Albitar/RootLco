Every finding produced by the workshop archaeology, assigned to the phase that
owns it, with one question answered for each: **does it block the P1-27 gate?**

## The rule this document applies

A finding blocks `P1-G27` **only** if it prevents one of the 42 canonical P1-27
tasks — CRM 16, Vehicles 13, Security 4, QA 5, DevOps 2, Documentation 2 — from
meeting its Definition of Done.

It does **not** block P1-27 because it is severe, because it was discovered
during P1-27, or because Reception, Work Orders, technicians, parts, quotations,
billing, QA or delivery need it. Those belong to P1-28 … P1-31 and are recorded
here as theirs.

**Reclassifying ownership downgrades nothing.** A Critical owned by P1-30 is
still Critical for P1-30.

## What this corrects

An earlier reading measured the whole workshop journey against P1-27's boundary
and reported "0 of 23 waves buildable". That number was true of the _journey_ and
misleading about _P1-27_: most of what it counted was never P1-27's to deliver.
The correct P1-27 statement is below, and it is neither the optimistic one nor
the alarming one.

## How it was produced, and how far to trust it

Thirteen parallel classifiers, one per archaeology domain, each followed by an
**adversarial challenger with the opposite brief** — hunting for findings wrongly
cleared as "a later phase" _and_ findings wrongly called P1-27 blockers. Both
errors are costly: a false clear ships a broken CRM or Vehicle screen, a false
block holds a finished phase open for work that is not its own.

Twelve challenges overturned nothing. One overturned a verdict — `VHM-15`, whose
own evidence recorded a canonical Definition-of-Done failure and then filed it as
neutral background. That overturn is applied.

## Why the automated tiers were all green

Nothing below was caught by 1687 unit, 1647 database, 1834 backend or 146
browser tests, nor by any of the nine policy gates. The reason is consistent:

- **A registered operation with no call site is invisible to every gate.** The
  authorization-coverage check asks whether a route exists for a registration and
  whether a registration exists for a route. Neither asks whether the product
  ever calls it.
- **A raw UUID passes the plain-language gate**, because the gate reads catalogue
  strings and the UUID arrives at runtime.
- **A millisecond cursor paginates correctly** whenever no two rows share a
  millisecond, which is every fixture that was not built to collide.

The phase's own traceability already recorded "Not called" against three of these
operations. The information existed; nothing was asked to act on it.
